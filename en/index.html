What happened

Hello — we are Mabel Xiomara's family and friends: her cousin Natalia, together with relatives and friends.

On August 10, 2026 at 7:34 in the morning, the magnitude 7.4 earthquake that shook Colombia severely damaged Mabel Xiomara's house in the La Pilastra neighbourhood of Circasia, Quindío. She lived there with her three sons — Matías (14), Gabriel and Joel (10) — and her partner, Arlex, the boys' father.

It is the house Mabel Xiomara built just two years ago, with her savings and two personal loans. The family lived on the upper floor while the ground floor was still being finished. The earthquake cracked columns and walls on that ground floor. After the technical inspection by the Disaster Risk Management Council, the mayor's office officially notified Mabel Xiomara that the house is UNINHABITABLE and had to be evacuated. They cannot go back — and the ground is not stable enough to rebuild there. The family has to start over somewhere else.

The loans were personal loans, with no insurance against natural disasters. Mabel Xiomara must keep paying $1,824,000 COP (≈ €400) every month — for a house she can no longer live in. The outstanding debt: nearly 90 million pesos (≈ €19,800), across two loans of 29 and 60 million.

Today all five of them live in one room at her grandmother's house.

Who Mabel Xiomara is

Many of you know her: for more than a decade, every December, Mabel Xiomara has collected donations from family and friends to bring Christmas gifts to children with disabilities at the INFAC foundation. She has spent over ten years asking on behalf of others. Now it's our turn to help her — to pay off the debt buried under the rubble, so she can start a new housing plan for her sons without chains.
