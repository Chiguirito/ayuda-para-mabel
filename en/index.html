What happened

Hello — this is Natalia, Mabel Xiomara's cousin, writing together with close family and friends.

On 10 August 2026, at 7:34 am, the magnitude 7.4 earthquake that shook Colombia severely damaged Mabel Xiomara's house in the La Pilastra neighbourhood of Circasia, Quindío. She lived there with her three sons — Matías (14) and the twins Gabriel and Joel (10) — and her partner, Arlex, the boys' father.

It is the house Mabel Xiomara built just two years ago, with her savings and two personal loans. The family lived on the upper floor while the ground floor was still being finished. The earthquake cracked columns and walls on that ground floor. After the technical inspection by the Municipal Disaster Risk Management Council, the mayor's office officially notified Mabel Xiomara that the house was declared UNINHABITABLE and had to be evacuated. They cannot go back — and the mayor's office also notified them that they cannot rebuild on the same plot. The family has to start over somewhere else.

The loans were personal loans, with no insurance against natural disasters. Mabel Xiomara must keep paying 1,824,000 COP (≈ €500) every month — for a house she can no longer live in. The outstanding debt: 89.4 million pesos (≈ €24,600), across two loans of roughly 29 and 60 million.

Today, all five of them sleep in a single room at Mabel Xiomara's grandmother's house.

Meet Mabel Xiomara

Many of you know her: for more than a decade, every December, Mabel Xiomara has collected donations from family and friends to bring Christmas gifts to children with disabilities at the INFAC foundation. She has spent more than ten years asking for help for others. Now it's our turn to ask for her — to pay off the debt buried under the rubble, so she can start again and build a new home for her sons, free of that burden.

Transparency

  • Donations in Colombia go directly to the family — Mabel Xiomara's Bancolombia account or her Nequi — no intermediaries.
  • PayPal, Alipay and bank-transfer donations are received by Natalia (her cousin, who lives in China), who forwards everything to Mabel Xiomara and posts the receipts on this page.
  • The debt is two personal loans with payments of 608,000 and 1,216,000 COP per month — 1,824,000 COP in total; combined balance: 89.4 million COP.
  • Why doesn't insurance cover this? Because these are personal loans, not a mortgage: unlike Colombian mortgages — which by law include fire and earthquake insurance — a personal loan carries no coverage on the house. There is no policy to activate.
  • Why our own page and not GoFundMe? GoFundMe cannot pay out to Colombia, and platforms charge fees — this page was built by the family and deducts nothing.
